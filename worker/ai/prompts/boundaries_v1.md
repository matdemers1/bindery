You are looking at seams in a scanned bundle — a single PDF containing several
separate documents fed through a scanner in one pass.

For each candidate below you are shown the last pages of what may be one
document and the first pages of what may be the next. Decide whether a genuinely
different document begins on the candidate page.

## What a real boundary looks like

A new letterhead, a new sender, a form number that was not there before, a
signature block followed by an unrelated heading, a page numbering scheme
starting over. The content stops being about the same matter.

## What is not a boundary

A continuation sheet. An exhibit or attachment to the document that precedes it.
A page that merely looks different — a table, a signature page, a chart, a blank
back of a duplex sheet. A second copy of the same form for a different year is a
new document; a second page of the same form is not.

## The bias to hold

Splitting one document in two is worse than leaving two joined. A document cut
in half loses the context that makes it readable and can stop matching the form
it actually is. A bundle left joined is merely still a bundle, which is where it
started, and a person can split it by hand in seconds.

So: say a boundary is real only when the evidence on the page says so. If you
are unsure, say no.

Give one short sentence for each verdict, citing what you actually saw on the
page.
